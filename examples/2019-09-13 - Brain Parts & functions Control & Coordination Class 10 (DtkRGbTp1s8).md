## Summary: The brain's three-part structure maps to voluntary vs involuntary function

**TL;DR**: Forebrain handles all voluntary and conscious activity; midbrain and hindbrain control involuntary processes that keep you alive.

- **Three divisions**: forebrain (large outer mass), midbrain (brainstem start), hindbrain (lower brainstem + cerebellum)
- **Forebrain runs everything voluntary**: thinking, speech, memory, all five senses, emotions, hunger, thirst
- **Emotions originate in the forebrain, not the heart**: the heart is a pump
- **Medulla oblongata runs life-critical involuntary processes**: heartbeat, breathing, digestion, sneezing, coughing
- **Cerebellum handles balance and motor memory**: spatial coordination and learned motor skills like cycling or touch-typing
- **Alcohol impairs driving by hitting the cerebellum first**: balance and automated motor skills degrade before conscious thought does
- **Midbrain controls involuntary reflexes**: pupil constriction and automatic responses to stimuli

## Hidden Gems

- **The bicycle example cleanly separates forebrain from cerebellum**: active learning uses forebrain; once mastered, the skill transfers to cerebellum — explaining why experts "don't think about it"
