## Summary: Faro Shuffle + One-Handed Bridge

**TL;DR**: Split deck in half, align corners, sway halves to interweave every card, bridge.

1. Split — mechanic's grip, deck sideways; right index riffles off ~half.
2. Square — thumb and ring finger press side edges flush; align top edges.
3. Grip — right: thumb ¾ down, middle/ring on side, pinky at back. Left: thumb top-left, index on top, two fingers on side.
4. Sway — touch bottom corners only; sway right packet left-right until cards interweave.
5. Push — push packets together, loosen thumbs so cards slide fully in.
6. Bridge — left index under packet; grip both ends, feel tension, release.

## Hidden Gems

- Perfect Faro = exactly 26 per half — impressive, impractical; rough halves work.
- Left-right sway is the key: corners find the interleave path before full pressure.

## Comment Insights (Faro Shuffle)

### Failure Fixes Not in Video

- **Top half won't separate**: Ensure first card of each half starts interlocked as a guide before pushing the rest through.
- **Cards bending during bridge**: Bridge opposite to riffle direction to counteract and straighten.
- **Middle finger locks up**: Right hand index on top, remaining fingers to the back — not hanging over the edge toward the left packet.
- **Rounded corners block interlock**: MSC Modiano and similar decks prevent proper interlocking. Use Bicycle Rider Back, Tally-Ho, or Theory11.

### Community-Sourced Grip Detail (not in video)

- Left thumb ~¾ up the side, index curled toward bottom, pinky under back corner; thumb must not hang over top.
- Right packet diagonal corner pressure into left packet's back corner, slight clockwise rotation of right hand before applying pressure.
- Once a few cards weave: rotate right hand so card backs face down while pivoting away from body — riffles the rest through.

### Learning Curve Reality

- **Table-flat practice first**: Removes grip complexity, lets learners feel the interleave before going in-hand.
- **Knack moment is real**: Muscle memory, not understanding, is the limiter. Dozens of hours reported before it clicks.
- **Orientation swap when stuck**: Swap top/bottom packets or rotate 180° and retry.

### Perfect Faro Practicality Debate

One commenter disputes the "impractical" framing: exact 26/26 split enables mathematical card tricks (unshuffle, position control). Relevant for anyone pursuing cardistry beyond aesthetics.
